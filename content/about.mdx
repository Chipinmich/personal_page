## Short version

I'm a first-year computer science student at the University of Michigan. Most of
what I know, I learned by building something, breaking it, and then working out
why. This site is where that record lives: projects that got finished,
experiments that did not, and notes on what I was reading at the time.

Right now the thing holding most of my attention is
[training an AI to race in F-Zero](/projects/f-zero-ai) — an experiment in
genetic algorithms that has taught me more about writing a good fitness function
than about racing.

## Education

**University of Michigan** — Computer Science, first year

I'm just getting started on the coursework, so most of what's on this site came
from building things on my own rather than from a class. I expect that balance to
shift, and I plan to keep documenting both.

## What I'm interested in

- **Machine learning.** Especially the parts where you can watch the learning
  happen rather than reading it off a chart.
- **Robotics and control.** The gap between what a model assumes and what the
  world does.
- **Systems and software engineering.** Writing code that someone else — usually
  me, six months later — can still work with.
- **Game AI.** A surprisingly good testbed, because failure is obvious.

## Current focus

- Getting genuinely comfortable with genetic algorithms and reinforcement
  learning rather than just familiar with the vocabulary
- Building the habit of documenting experiments while they run, not afterwards
- Reading more primary sources and fewer summaries of them

## Longer term

I want to work on systems that have to operate in the real world — robotics,
autonomy, or applied machine learning — where the interesting problems come from
the environment being messier than the model. Before that, I want a lot more
practice at the unglamorous part: making experiments reproducible and being
honest about what the results actually show.
